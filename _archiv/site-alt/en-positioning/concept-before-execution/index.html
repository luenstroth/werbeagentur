Concept before execution: decisions are the service, not drafts

What to do is the hard question. What it looks like is the follow-up question.

Execution is no longer a scarce resource. Design, copy, images, layout, code — all of this has become fast and cheap in recent years, and the trend will not reverse. An agency that positions itself on execution positions itself against a competitor that never tires and costs almost nothing.

What is not available automatically is the decision about what should be executed — and the responsibility for that decision. That is why at Lünstroth diagnosis and concept come first, rather than running alongside as unpaid groundwork.

Diagnosis and concept are services in their own right with a defined scope — not unbilled groundwork for a design commission.

What a concept is — and what it is not

What is often called a concept

A collection of ideas. A mood board. Three proposals to choose from. A presentation that shows possibilities and hands the decision back to the client.

The tell-tale sign: there is no rationale that could be checked. So taste gets negotiated — and the approval committee decides by what it likes.

What concept means here

A well-founded decision about what is done and what is left out, with a rationale that can be traced from the decision situation through to the means.

The tell-tale sign: you can disagree with it. Not because you dislike something, but because an assumption is wrong. That is a productive argument.

Change follows strategy

The primacy of the concept leads to a second principle that runs against expectations even more often in practice: what the strategy demands is changed — nothing more. Recognition is an economic asset built up over years, and giving it up means writing it off. You do that for a reason or not at all.

Classic design studios often start thinking about a new logo before anyone has asked for one — a relaunch is a visible result and easy to sell. The analysis, however, regularly leads to a different answer:

  • Gneuss: the logo was not the problem, a single element of it was. The symbol went, the word mark stayed unchanged.
  • Volksbank PHD: the acquired local brand was kept in full. An endorsement instead of a merger — dissolving it would have cost more than it brought.
  • Cobiax: here the change was genuinely necessary. And even then the characteristic extension of the “x” was kept.
  • Meco: adapted to digital viewing habits without leaving the existing character of the logo.

Four mandates, four different depths of intervention — and in three of the four, the most valuable part of the brand was left untouched. That is no coincidence but the result of the same question: does the existing form still carry the strategy?

The four stages — and why the order is not negotiable

Each stage can be commissioned individually. None can be skipped without the next one building on assumptions instead of findings.

A

Diagnosis

Market position, competitive environment, sales channels, pricing rationale, legal situation. The result is a description of the status quo that includes uncomfortable findings too. Fixed scope, can be commissioned on its own.

B

Concept

The well-founded decision: what to do, what to leave out, in which order, with what expected effect. This is also where the depth of intervention in the existing brand is decided.

C

Execution

Brand and identity, media that sell: packaging, sales and listing documents, website, video, specialist communication. Every medium follows from the concept and can be checked against it.

D

Operation

Ongoing care of the brand system, content and product data, brand rules for the client's AI systems, technical operation. A brand that is not maintained falls apart into its components.

What it costs — and what it saves

Paying for a concept phase without seeing anything is hard. The objection is fair, and the answer to it is not a promise but a calculation: rounds of revisions are the most expensive item in communication projects. They arise when drafts are discussed for which there is no rationale — then hierarchy takes the place of argument, and compromise the place of decision.

Once a concept exists, the discussion moves one level forward: you argue about assumptions, not about taste. That argument is shorter because it can be settled — and it takes place before production, not after.
