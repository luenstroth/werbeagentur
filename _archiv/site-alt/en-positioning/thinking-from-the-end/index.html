Thinking from the end: developing tasks backwards from their goal

Ask the last question first — and work backwards from there.

Most communication projects start at the front: with the means. A campaign, a website relaunch, a trade fair stand, a new logo. The tool is fixed before the task is clear — and what follows is the search for a justification for a decision already made.

Thinking from the end means reversing the order. The starting point is not the question of what should be produced, but a different one: what has to happen at the end — and who has to believe what for it to happen? Only once this question is answered does it follow, backwards, what needs to be done. Sometimes that is a pack. Sometimes a single sentence. Sometimes the decision not to touch the existing logo.

The means is the last thing to be settled — not the first.

The decision situation as the starting point

All communication aims at a moment in which someone makes a decision. That moment is concrete, not abstract: it has a place, a duration, an alternative and a person who knows one thing and believes another. If you cannot describe it, you cannot work towards it.

Four questions clarify it:

  • Who actually decides? Not who signs, but who prepares the decision — and who can overturn it.
  • Where, and within what time? Three seconds at the chiller cabinet is a different task from a six-month tender process.
  • Against which alternative? “Doing nothing” is an alternative too — often the strongest.
  • What does this person know at this moment, and what do they believe? The gap between the two is the real task.

Backwards through the chain

From the decision situation a path leads back, and it has more stages than a brief usually allows for. Between manufacturer and end customer stand purchasing, retail, specialist planners, recommenders, testing bodies — and each of these stages assesses the same offer by its own criteria.

An argument that convinces purchasing can leave the end customer cold. A technical advantage that a specialist planner understands can seem meaningless or even disadvantageous to the developer. Working backwards means clarifying, for each stage, what has to get through there — and through which carrier it can get through.

Only at the end of this chain comes the medium. That is why what will be produced is not fixed when a mandate starts. Anyone who knows it in advance has not analysed the task but anticipated it.

Two cases in which the reversal tipped the balance

Cobiax: the decision was not made by the specialist at all

The previous claim addressed structural engineers — technically correct, since they can assess the construction method. But they rarely decide alone. Seen from the end, the real decision lay with the developer, and for the developer a lighter floor slab was not an advantage but rather a cause for suspicion: heavier is considered more solid. The advantage that carried lay elsewhere — longer spans, so fewer columns, so more design freedom. A different claim emerged from this backwards view, not from better wording.

Kühlmann: three seconds at the chiller cabinet

For a retailer brand in the discount channel there is no campaign, no advertisement, no sales visit. The pack is the only sales tool, and the decision situation lasts seconds. Thinking from the end, everything else follows: a constant brand header for recognition, three colours that organise the range without reading, fixed positions for net quantity and fat content. None of these decisions is a design preference. Each is the consequence of a decision situation that was described first.

What this means for working together

This order has practical consequences, and not only pleasant ones. It begins with a phase in which there is nothing to see yet: diagnosis and concept. Anyone expecting quick drafts experiences it as a delay. In fact it shortens the overall path, because it removes the rounds of revisions that arise when taste is negotiated instead of a rationale.

It also has an uncomfortable side: sometimes the analysis shows that the commissioned means is not the right one. A relaunch that turns out to be unnecessary. A campaign that does not solve the problem. Saying so is part of the service — it is the point at which an agency that works conceptually differs from a supplier that executes what was ordered.

More on positioning

Once the goal is set, the decision follows about what is done and what is left out — concept before execution. And the question of where the brand should stand in the end: placement in the market.
